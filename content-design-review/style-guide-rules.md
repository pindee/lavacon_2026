# Example Style Guide Rules (Template)
> **These are examples of one team's standards, not universal rules. Don't copy them as they are.** This file is a **starter rule set** for the content-design-review skill. The rules reflect common content design conventions plus some house-style choices (for example, ALL CAPS buttons and hyphenated ranges). Edit, delete, or replace anything here to match your own style guide.

---

## Brand Voice

Three core voice pillars:

- **Approachable** — Explain things simply, in the language of our customers, without underestimating their knowledge. Avoid excessive jargon. Invite engagement and provide learning resources.
- **Authentic** — Say what we mean. Communicate with directness and substance. Avoid overly casual or aggressive sales language. Include clear calls to action reflecting real value.
- **Informed** — Speak from deep industry knowledge and genuine understanding of customer challenges. Empower decision-making with actionable insights.

---

## Tone (Context-Dependent)

Tone adapts while voice stays consistent. Your organization can use the Nielsen Norman four dimensions: Humor, Formality, Respectfulness, Enthusiasm.


## Active Voice

- Always prefer active voice — especially in procedural content.
- Active voice is shorter, clearer, and translates better across languages.
- Replace passive verbs with active ones; convert nouns into verbs.

---

## Anthropomorphism

- Computers do not think, look, or listen. Programs are inanimate.
- Avoid possessive constructions with non-human objects.
- Center on user actions, not software agency.

---

## Plain Language

**Is:**
- Explaining confusing terms or jargon when necessary
- Simplifying words and ideas for a general audience
- Making content clear the first time it's read
- Using common, everyday words

**Is NOT:**
- Leaving out necessary technical terms
- Writing in a patronizing or condescending tone
- Over-explaining everything

**Techniques:** shorter sentences, bullets, scannable text, chunking, familiar vocabulary. Target 5th–7th grade reading level.

---

## Sentence Length and Structure

- Maximum sentence length: 25 words; 20 or fewer is better.
- One thought per sentence: clear subject + verb + object.
- Avoid complex sentences with multiple dependent/independent clauses.
- Shorter sentences also aid translation (e.g., German expands during translation).

---

## Present Tense

- Use present tense for general statements, descriptions, and instructions.
- Use past/future tense only when conveying specific time-related information.

---


## First-Person vs. Second-Person

- **"My"** — Use when the interface displays items belonging to the user (ownership/personalization).
  - Examples: "My Messages," "My Profile," "My Tasks"
- **"Your"** — Use when the system addresses the user directly in instructional contexts.
  - Examples: "Update your profile"
- Never mix "My" and "Your" in the same context.
- Avoid third-person constructions (his/her) or referring to "the reader/the user" generically.

---

## Parallelism

- Express parallel thoughts in grammatically parallel syntax.
- Apply across body text, headings, lists, procedures, and tables.
- If one item in a series begins with a verb, all items must begin with a verb.

---

## Headings and Titles

### Structure Rules
- Use parallel wording at parallel heading levels
- Always have body text between headings
- Never skip heading levels (e.g., don't jump H1 → H3)
- Don't start a heading with "A," "An," or "The"
- Don't use reserved terms (Figure Caption, Table Title, Column Heading) as section headings
- All headings/titles use Title Case

### Heading Hierarchy (Accessibility)
- H1: page title only — use once per page
- H2–H6: sections and subsections in logical hierarchy
- H2: use for dialog box headings (H1 already exists on main page)
- Never select heading level based on visual size
- Never use headings purely for visual emphasis

### Help File Specifics
- Begin topic titles with gerund verbs (e.g., "Managing Order Templates")
- Section headings must differ from the topic title
- In Overview documents: label first conceptual section "Overview"

---

## Acronyms

- **First use:** Spell out the full phrase followed by the acronym in parentheses.
  - Example: "Customer Relationship Management (CRM)"
- **Industry-standard acronyms** (for example, API, PDF, SSO): can stand alone after first use
- **Overview sections:** always spell out even if industry-standard
- **Headings/titles:** limit or avoid acronyms
- **Marketing (organic search):** spell out terms

---


## Punctuation

**Apostrophes:** Possessives on singular nouns ending in s/x/z get 's (e.g., "boss's"). Never use with possessive pronouns. Don't use with company/product names.

**Commas:** Use Oxford comma in lists (e.g., "save, edit, or delete").

**Bullets:**
- If bullets complete a sentence → end each with a period
- If bullets are a simple item list → no periods or commas

**Hyphens:** Use for ranges (including day, date, and most numeric ranges), compound words, and title separators. Capitalize both words in Title Case (e.g., "Checked-In").
- Day range: `Sunday-Saturday`
- Date range: `January-March`, `01/01/2026-03/31/2026`
- Numeric/age range: `18-24 years`

**En dash (–):** Reserved for specific typographic cases — *not* the default for ranges in product copy.
- Empty table cells: `–`
- Numeric ranges that share a prefix/suffix in technical contexts: `1110–40 lines` (style-guide example).
- For everyday day/date/numeric ranges in UI copy, use a **hyphen** (see above).

**Em dash (—):** Replaces semicolon/colon informally. No spaces around it.

**Parentheses:** Enclose nonessential info. Lowercase first word unless it's a complete sentence. Closing punctuation goes outside.

**Semicolons:** Avoid. Replace with periods to create separate sentences.

**Ampersand (&):** Never use & in UI copy, body text, headings, labels, or button text. Always spell out "and." Reason: "and" is more universally understood and easier to localize than &. Exception: third-party brand names that officially use & (e.g., AT&T).

**Colons:** Always follow a colon with a single space. This applies to alert/banner prefixes (`Warning: You have used…`), inline labels, and any other colon usage in UI copy. Do not run text directly after a colon with no space (`Warning:You have used…`).

**Forward slashes:**
- "Or" relationship: no spaces (Admin/Billing)
- "And" relationship: spaces before/after
- Industry terms: no spaces (A/P)
- Character count: no spaces (32/250)
- Delimiters: spaces before/after (Account ID / Account Name)

---


## Lists

**Bulleted lists:**
- Use for 2+ items where order doesn't matter
- Keep items brief — 3 lines maximum
- Don't use a list for a single item

**Numbered lists:**
- Use for items where order matters but it's not a procedure
- For procedures in your CCMS (for example, Paligo): use the procedure tag, not a numbered list

**Descriptive lists:**
- Use a dash to separate the item name from its description
- Example: "Team Calendar - Shows events scheduled for your team"

**Procedures with alternatives:**
- Use a numbered step + sub-bullets: "1. Do one of the following:" followed by bulleted options

---

## Corporate Names and Branding

- Always use the full, official company name. Never abbreviate it or write it in all caps.
- Avoid the possessive form of the company name. Rewrite the sentence instead ("The team at YourCompany...").
- First reference to a product: full name with definite article ("the YourCompany Dashboard"). Subsequent mentions: "the Dashboard"
- External products: use generic terminology rather than trademarks unless necessary

---

## Product Names

- Use the full, correct product name when first mentioning a product
- Avoid possessives attached to product names (rewrite sentences instead)
- Example: "Document Manager has new features" not "Document Manager's new features"

---

## Versions

- Use "v" to denote version numbers: "Acrobat v9"
- Earlier versions: "Acrobat v9 or earlier"
- Later versions: "Acrobat v9 or later"

---

## Compliance

**Non-confidential documents:**
- Include a legal notice with disclaimers about accuracy and reliability
- State information is provided "as is" without warranties

**Confidential documents:**
- Include legal notice plus additional protective language
- State proprietary nature and restrictions on copying/distribution
- Specify owner's legal rights

---

## International Language

- Use language sensitive to a global audience
- Avoid jargon or technical terms that may not be widely understood
- Use the same UI terminology in technical documentation as appears in the interface
- Avoid phrasing that may be offensive or contain cultural bias
- Reference the Localization section for market-specific adaptations

---

## Localization

- Replace complex terminology with simpler alternatives ("Use" not "Utilize")
- Use concise sentence structures to reduce translation cost
- Reuse identical phrasing across similar concepts for consistent translation
- Avoid embedding text in screen captures — use numbered callouts instead
- Use closed captions derived from scripts (avoids re-recording audio)
- Maintain consistent terminology; reuse content to minimize translation effort
- Adapt colors, images, and symbols for cultural differences
- Use local formats for dates, currency, time, and measurement units
- Test localized products with user feedback

---

## Mobile Devices

- Use mobile-specific terminology: "tap," "swipe," "dock"
- Use "tap" not "click" for touch interactions
- Keep content short — short sentences and short paragraphs
- Items are "in" the Dock, not "on" it; don't use "docked" as a verb

---

## Accessibility

Based on **WCAG 2.0** (perceivable, operable, understandable, robust).

- Write at grade 5–7 reading level
- Use simple sentences in present tense
- Avoid metaphors and complex phrasing; use Hemingway Editor to verify
- Provide descriptive alt text for all images and icons — answer "What information does this convey?"
- Leave alt text empty for decorative images or those with captions
- Add closed captions to all videos; use `<Music only>` or `<Video only, no audio>` as needed
- Maintain 4.5:1 color contrast ratio (normal text); 3:1 for large text
- Never rely on color alone to convey information
- Eliminate locational descriptors (above/below) unless specific
- Never communicate information solely through graphics — restate in captions, tables, or paragraphs
- Provide contextual link text — avoid one-word hyperlinks
- No "enable/disable" → use "activate/deactivate" or "turn on/turn off"
- Alt text for clickable icons: use aria-label (e.g., `aria-label="Download File"`)
- Non-clickable icons (e.g., search icon in search field): require alt-text via engineering

---

## Inclusivity

**Disability language:**
- "enable/disable" → "activate/deactivate" or "turn on/turn off"
- Avoid: "blind spot," "handicap," "suffers from," "normal/abnormal"

**Gender-neutral language:**
- Use singular "they," "everyone," "people," or specific nouns instead of gendered pronouns
- "mother/father" → "parent or guardian"
- "manmade" → "artificial or synthetic"
- "partner or spouse" instead of gender-specific terms

**Race-inclusive language:**
- "blacklist/whitelist" → "deny list/allow list"
- "master" → "primary"
- "grandfathered" → "legacy"
- "tribe" → "friends"
- "white glove" → "premium service"
- "low-hanging fruit" → "quick wins"

## Responsible AI (RAI) Content Standards

Six pillars:

1. **Fairness** — Avoid biased or exclusionary terms. Communicate fairness-related issues clearly.
2. **Reliability** — Communicate AI limitations and performance metrics to prevent overconfidence.
3. **Privacy** — Never expose sensitive data in examples or UI text.
4. **Accountability** — Attribute AI decisions and provide escalation paths.
5. **Transparency** — Explain AI functionality, risks, and intended use in plain language.
6. **Human Oversight** — Encourage users to validate AI outputs and maintain professional judgment.

**Do:** Provide disclaimers, use labels/icons for AI-driven features, layer content (summary + detail link), use inclusive language.

**Don't:** Use vague terms ("smart," "intelligent") without context, conceal AI involvement, suggest AI replaces professional expertise.

---

## Conversation Design (AI Chatbots)

Chatbot personality traits: Task-focused, Organized, Confident, Polite, Responsible.

**Design principles:**
- Minimize user effort; maximize clarity
- Turn-based: pose clear questions; respond to every interaction with greeting and farewell
- Context-aware: personalize based on user behavior
- Error tolerance: anticipate mistakes with polite recovery options
- Responses: under 200 words maximum
- Maintain sentence case with standard punctuation
- Use contractions for conversational tone
- Avoid emojis, exclamation marks, colloquialisms

**Clarification flow (3 levels):**
1. Apologize and repeat question
2. Apologize and show capabilities
3. Escalate to human support

**Guardrails:** Never guess or fabricate information. Respect privacy. Plain language. No bias or assumptions.

## Product Components — Detailed Rules

### Alt Text / Aria Labels
- Alt text: use for all images including non-clickable icons
- Leave alt text **empty** for decorative images or images with captions
- Aria labels: use for clickable icons or buttons without visible labels
- Don't use "image of..." or "graphic of..." in descriptions

### App Bar
- Title Case for product name and account or site name
- Implement ARIA attributes and hover text for icon buttons
- Never abbreviate product names (no "RW" for "Reports Workspace")
- No punctuation marks in app bar labels

### Buttons
- ALL CAPS formatting
- 2 words maximum; 3 only when essential
- Action-first wording
- Button text should align with the dialog title in dialog boxes

**Buttons vs. links:** Use buttons when actions modify backend/frontend systems. Use links for navigation without affecting site structure.

### Checkboxes / Radio Buttons
- **Checkboxes:** multiselect — any number of choices including zero
- **Radio buttons:** single select — exactly one choice from 2+ mutually exclusive options

### Dialog Boxes
- Use sparingly — they are intentionally interruptive
- Explain "what" in the title, "why" in the body
- Button text must match the dialog action/title
- Avoid apologies, rhetorical questions, vague buttons ("Yes," "Sure," "OK")

### Dropdowns / Lists
- Begin each list item with a capital letter
- Proper nouns: Title Case, no punctuation
- Full sentences/questions: Sentence case with periods or question marks
- Parallel structure required — if one item starts with a verb, all must

### Emails
**General rules:**
- Use the Brand Email Template
- Include personalization (recipient's name in subject/body)
- One clear primary CTA button
- Minimum 11pt font; left-align text
- Short paragraphs with single focus
- No secondary CTAs that compete with the primary action

**Email types:**
- Auto Response: state the address isn't monitored; provide alternative contacts
- Password Reset: neutral tone; conditional messaging for non-initiators; include time-limited link
- Password Change Confirmation: confirm change; alert user if they didn't authorize it
- Registration/Create Account: state benefits; include creation link with expiration; provide support contact
- Welcome Email: thank, remind, provide sign-in link
- Unsubscribe: include for anti-spam compliance

### Empty States
- Format: "To [value], [name the task]"
- Use your brand voice and tone
- Clearly state purpose without implying immediate actions
- Avoid multiple options; focus on the most important action
- Use en dash (–) for empty table cells

### Error States
- Use clear, concise language; provide specific next steps
- Only display error codes when required for customer support — always explain why
- Never use snackbars for error messages (snackbars = success/non-urgent only)

**Common field validation error messages:**
- "Required"
- "Cannot be future date"
- "End date must be after start date"
- "Enter mm/dd/yyyy"
- "Enter a 10-digit phone number"
- "Enter letters or numbers"
- "Maximum XXX characters"
- "Incorrect verification code. Try again or request a new code."

**Full-page error types:** Security error, Page loading error with code, Page not found, Product unavailable

### Fields
- Title Case for all field labels (e.g., "First Name")
- Single-select fields: singular labels
- Multi-select: singular if only one applies per result; plural if multiple can apply
- Indicate required fields clearly
- Include character counters for text fields when users will likely exceed limits
- Space between field name and required asterisk
- Don't rely on asterisk alone — include explanatory text ("* indicates a required field")

### Filters
- "Filter" (singular) or "Filters" (plural) for dialog title
- Single-select: singular label; multi-select: singular or plural based on results
- Always include Apply button and Clear Filters button
- Use checkboxes for multiple simultaneous filter selections
- Use chips to display applied filters

### Ghost Text
- 5 words or fewer
- Begin with an action verb
- Label and ghost text should complement, not duplicate each other
- Include formatting examples when helpful

Examples: "Enter your email address," "Search customer name, order, or invoice"

### Icons
- **Clickable with label:** label appears below icon
- **Clickable without label:** add hover text + aria label via engineering
- **Non-clickable:** add alt-text via engineering; no hover text needed
- In help files: include the word "icon" (e.g., "Click the more icon")
- Use icons sparingly in technical documentation

### Images
- Always precede images with introductory text
- Use proper alt text and aria labels
- Keep captions concise and descriptive
- Never rely solely on images — always accompany with text
- Don't include visible mouse cursors in screenshots unless critical
- Don't embed text in images (increases translation cost)

### Information Messages
- Provide enough information to understand why to act or not act
- Include results/consequences of user actions
- Use examples to clarify concepts
- Make CTAs clear and explicit — users must know what happens when they click

### Link Text
- Minimum 2 words
- Action-focused language showing what users will accomplish
- Describe destination content or action clearly

**Link styling:**
- List links: underline on hover only
- Standalone links: underline on hover only
- Inline links: permanent underline to distinguish from body text

### Microcopy
- 1 line only — positioned directly beneath the relevant field
- Sentence case
- Must add value; avoid stating the obvious

Examples: "Comments entered in this field are visible to your team," "Run report to see most recent results"

### Navigation Drawers
- Title Case for all labels
- Parallel structure — all items must follow the same grammatical pattern
- No unnecessary punctuation

### Password / Sign-In / Log-In Screens
- Standard format: two fields (email/username + password)
- Use "sign in" / "sign out" (not "log in" / "log out")
- Always include "Forgot password?" link with specific action text
- Error messages: keep generic for security ("Incorrect username or password")
- Display validation errors above the username field

**Password requirements (example):** Minimum 10 characters; at least one uppercase, one lowercase, one number; cannot include username or name.

### Search Fields
- Sentence case
- Placeholder text with examples of what users can search for
- Use "Search" label when there are more than 3 searchable items
- Never list 4+ items in placeholder text

### Security Access
- State access requirements clearly and positively
- Focus on what users *can* do and what steps to take
- Never blame users for lacking permissions

### Session Timeout
- "Timeout" = one word, no hyphen
- Conversational tone for already-timed-out sessions
- Buttons: "CONTINUE SESSION" and "SIGN OUT" (not "OK" or "CANCEL")
- Inform users when their work was saved

### Steppers
- Clear, concise language for each step
- Provide clear progress indication
- Clearly labeled, visible, distinguishable buttons
- Support keyboard navigation
- Display visible error messages with remediation guidance

### Tables
- Numerical content and column headers: **right-aligned**
- Textual, date content and column headers: **left-aligned**
- Precede every table with introductory text
- Place table immediately after its introduction
- Use en dash (–) for nil or unknown values
- Invert tables with many columns and few rows for readability
- Titles: keep concise and descriptive (typically not used)

### Toggles
- Label with the noun or feature it represents (e.g., "Secure Messaging Access")
- Title Case for labels (unless in a complete sentence)
- States: "On" / "Off" inside the toggle
- Instruction format: "Turn on [Label Name]"
- Avoid labels that describe resulting states ("Access enabled") or include action verbs ("Enable X")

### Tooltips
- **Plain tooltips:** label icon buttons on hover
- **Rich tooltips:** supplementary info or actions for UI elements or new features
- Display only one tooltip at a time
- Title Case for most tooltips; Sentence case only for complete sentences
- Add period when tooltip text is a full sentence
- Don't use plain tooltips when the UI element already has a label
- Don't place critical information exclusively in tooltips
- Never wrap tooltip text to multiple lines or include excessive information

### Warning Messages
- Use strong words: "must," "never," "cannot"
- Clearly state potential consequences and irreversible actions
- Provide enough detail for users to understand risks before proceeding
- Avoid ambiguous phrasing that permits multiple interpretations

Examples:
- "Warning: 4 tasks are overdue by more than 1 month." [VIEW]
- "Warning: You are viewing sensitive account information."

## Customer Communications

### Help Files
- Empathy, simplicity, effectiveness, and focus
- Action-oriented content that directly addresses customer needs
- Scannable formatting with concise, focused information
- Concise titles, brief summaries, and positive positioning
- Clearly outlined steps with notes, callouts, and warnings
- Use bold and italics for emphasis only; links must be strategic
- Avoid images unless necessary for process comprehension
- Avoid sales-oriented language
- Never list limitations without proposing solutions

### Interactive Assistance and Walkthroughs
- Clear and concise language in each step
- Logical, sequential flow easy to navigate
- Provide visual cues (arrows, highlights) for specific elements
- Use tooltips/pop-ups for additional context
- Include interactive elements (buttons, checkboxes) to engage users
- Test thoroughly for seamless UX
- Avoid: excessive information, technical jargon, overly long walkthroughs

### News and Updates Announcements
- Clear, direct, covering necessary details
- Maintain upbeat perspective highlighting benefits and solutions
- Provide explicit next steps for required customer actions
- Emphasize value and how updates improve experience
- Be transparent and honest; center the customer's perspective
- Include links to supporting materials (tooltips, guided tours, info icons)
- Avoid: technical jargon, emphasizing drawbacks, overstating capabilities, condescending language

### Product Update Documents
- Empathize with customers — updates can feel overwhelming
- Scannable, navigable formatting
- Positive, constructive tone focused on resolutions, not limitations
- Highlight benefits and value of updates
- Avoid: excessive information, guesswork/vague explanations, sales-driven messaging

### Release Tooltips (for example, Pendo or similar in-app guides)
- Relevant, focused, and helpful — on-the-spot assistance only
- Action-oriented language addressing the user's question or concern
- Keep concise and scannable
- Positive tone; never just list limitations
- Not for upselling — support users functionally
- Review and update regularly for accuracy
